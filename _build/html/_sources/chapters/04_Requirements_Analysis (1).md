22
A set of tests is conducted to determine each requirement's validity.
1. Is the requirement traceable to a user need or operational requirement? 
2. Is the requirement redundant with any other requirement? 
3. Is the requirement consistent with other requirements? 
4. Is the requirement unambiguous and not subject to interpretation? 
5. Is the requirement technologically feasible?
6. Is the requirement affordable?
7. Is the requirement verifiable?
\n\n\n\n![Image](../images/04_Requirements_Analysis (1)_p22_i33.png)\n\nRequirements Analysis

2.
Is the set of requirements feasible in terms of cost, schedule, and technology?
3.
Can the set of requirements be verified as a whole?

\n\n\n\n![Image](../images/04_Requirements_Analysis (1)_p24_i35.png)\n\nRequirements metrics
25
• Metrics are used to inform the analyst, engineer, and decision 
maker whether the system will successfully accomplish its intended 
objective while in an operational environment under operational 
conditions
• Measure of effectiveness (MOE): A qualitative or quantitative 
metric of a system’s overall performance that indicates the degree 
to which it achieves its objectives under specified conditions. An 
MOE always refers to the system as a whole
• Measure of performance (MOP): A quantitative metric of a 
system’s characteristics or performance of a particular attribute or 
subsystem. An MOP typically measures a level of physical 
performance below that of the system as a whole

\n\n\n\n![Image](../images/04_Requirements_Analysis (1)_p26_i37.png)\n\nRequirements verification and validation
27
• Requirement verification is establishing confidence that the requirement has 
been met
o Requirements are verified by test, demonstration, analysis or inspection
o Test is the most common method of verification and the technique that provides the 
most confidence that the system will indeed work as intended in its anticipated 
environment
• Requirement validation is a process of ensuring that: 
o The set of requirements is correct, complete, and consistent,
o A model can be created that satisfies the requirements, and
o A real-world solution can be built and tested to prove that it satisfies the requirements
\n\n\n\n![Image](../images/04_Requirements_Analysis (1)_p27_i38.png)\n\nGoodness checklist: is this a requirement…?
28
q Dependent
q Verifiable
q Free of ambiguous terms?
q As appropriate, etc., and/or, support, but not limited to, be able to, be capable of
q Free of indefinite pronouns?
q This, these 
q Free of unverifiable terms?
q Flexible, user-friendly, robust, light-weight, maximize, adequate, small, portable, easily – other “ly” words and other “ize” words
q Free of implementation?
q Requirement should state WHAT is needed, NOT HOW to provide it, i.e., state the problem not the solution.
q Necessary?
q Ask “Why do you need the requirement?”; the answer may lead you to the real requirement.
q Free of descriptions of operations?
q To distinguish between operations and requirements, ask
q “Does the developer have control over this?”
q “Is this a need the product must satisfy or an activity involving the product?”
q Free of TBDs (To Be Determined)? 
q Use a best estimate and a TBR (To Be Resolved) with rationale when possible.
Ulrich and Eppinger, 2000
