• A risk rating is a measure of the potential inability to achieve overall program objectives 
within defined constraints, and has two components:
o The probability / likelihood of a risk being realized
o The consequence / impact if the risk is realized
• Likelihood rating scale (1 - 5) as defined by the NASA Engineering and Safety Center 
(NESC)
o 1 Very low (< 1E-06)
§
Example: fatal crashes per passenger airplane opportunity
o 2 Low (between 1E-06 and 1E-03)
§
Example: mechanical failure of proven design
o 3 Moderate (between 1E-03 and 1E-02)
§
Example: failure of first-time propulsive event
o 4 High (between 1E-02 and 1E-01)
§
Example: launch failure of development vehicle
o 5 Very likely (greater than 1E-01)
§
Example: rock impact when landing in densely populated boulder field

activities

• A systematic approach is taken toward risk identification/mitigation of a 
first-time activities and critical events. Through table-top reviews, address 
the following areas:
o Objectives. Purpose of the activity with a brief rationale
o Requirements/constraints. List all pertinent requirements, including windows of 
opportunity and needed DSN coverage
o Functional description. Include timeline, activity flow diagram, decision tree (if 
applicable), and predicted performance.
o Risk areas. Address risk areas and risk mitigations. Describe related spacecraft 
fault-protection response. Detail ground contingency plans and execution criteria
o Readiness assessment. Describe margins, spacecraft and vehicle readiness, flight 
team readiness, command product status, and documentation status.
• For all critical events (launch, TCMs, orbit insertion, EDL, aerobraking, 
major deployments), an independent review board holds a project-level 
review to assess readiness.
• Red team and external review teams are used throughout the process to 
assess the project’s readiness to perform critical activities and suggest 
potential improvements.
