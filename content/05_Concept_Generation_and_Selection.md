# Concept Generation and Selection

## 5.1 Expanding the Design Space
With the requirements rigidly defined, the systems engineering team transitions from defining the *problem* to defining the *solution*. Concept generation requires broad, unconstrained thinking to explore the entire design space. If the requirement is "travel 200km," the solution could be a fixed-wing aircraft, a quadcopter, a lighter-than-air blimp, or even a ground-based delivery robot.

## 5.2 The Morphological Matrix
A systematic approach to concept generation is the **Morphological Matrix**. The team lists all the primary functions the vehicle must perform (e.g., Generate Lift, Provide Propulsion, Store Energy) on the vertical axis. On the horizontal axis, they brainstorm every possible physical mechanism that could satisfy that function. By drawing lines through different combinations of these mechanisms, the team can systematically generate dozens of unique vehicle architectures.

## 5.3 Initial Screening and Feasibility
Not all generated concepts are viable. The initial down-selection process involves "sanity checks" against the laws of physics and hard constraints (e.g., maximum takeoff weight, cost ceilings). A heavy, lead-acid battery powered quadcopter might be immediately eliminated because it violates the range requirement, whereas a hybrid-electric fixed-wing design might proceed to the next phase.

## System Integration
When generating concepts for the RCUAV, the choice between Electric and Hybrid propulsion directly impacts the vehicle's mass fraction. Because 3D-printed structures typically have lower strength-to-weight ratios than composites, your concept selection must aggressively trade off propulsion weight against structural infill density using formal decision matrices.
